# Motorcycle-crash-detection-system
Crash system powered by an ESP32 that detects a crash on a motorcycle and sends the current location and makes an automatic all for help.


This bachelor's thesis addresses a critical issue in road safety by focusing on the design and implementation of an intelligent system for the active monitoring of motorcyclists. The specific dynamics of two-wheeled vehicles and the extreme vulnerability of riders during an impact demand the development of technical solutions capable of evaluating kinematic parameters in real time and automatically identifying accident scenarios. The proposed system utilizes a compact embedded architecture centered around the ESP32 microcontroller, whose computational resources enable local signal processing without relying on a permanent cloud connection.
The hardware foundation of the device integrates an MPU6050 inertial measurement unit, responsible for acquiring linear accelerations and angular velocities across three axes, alongside a global positioning module used to determine geographical coordinates and speed. To counteract the intense background noise caused by the structural vibrations of the internal combustion engine, a dual filtration strategy was deployed. This approach combines a register-level digital low-pass filter with a software algorithm based on a complementary filter, thereby achieving a precise and stable estimation of the roll angle.
The software component introduces an original contribution through the development of a hybrid detection algorithm structured hierarchically across two operational levels. The initial level monitors kinematic vectors through a deterministic model capable of instantly identifying high-energy mechanical collisions based on mathematical acceleration and jerk thresholds. When the parameters remain below these critical limits, the analysis is handed over to the second level, which consists of a machine learning model based on a one-dimensional convolutional neural network. This network was trained on an original experimental dataset, subsequently optimized using 8-bit quantization, and deployed directly on the microcontroller using TinyML technologies.
The experimental validation campaign involved navigating diverse urban routes and simulating controlled risk scenarios, proving a global accuracy of 97.3 percent in classifying riding states. The device demonstrated an excellent capacity to differentiate transient shocks caused by potholes or speed bumps from actual vehicle rollovers, with a local inference latency of under eight milliseconds. The obtained results confirm the technological viability of the proposed solution, paving the way toward the democratization of active safety systems in the motorcycle community.
